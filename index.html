Research profile

Mohamed Moussadek Maiza

Doctoral Researcher in Mathematics

Université de Sherbrooke · Québec, Canada

I am a doctoral researcher in mathematics at the Université de Sherbrooke, supervised by Jean-Philippe Burelle and Maxence Mayrand.

My research lies at the intersection of Poisson, Dirac, symplectic, and hyperkähler geometry, deformation theory, and moduli spaces. I study how geometric structures defined by Lie-group-valued moment maps relate to their Lie-algebra-valued counterparts, and how the resulting spaces behave under reduction and gluing.

My work also develops Hamiltonian and quasi-Hamiltonian field theories from quiver moduli, and hyperkähler moduli spaces arising from Nahm--Kirchhoff equations.

Research directions

Geometry across structures and scales

Poisson, Dirac, and deformation geometry

Poisson and Dirac structures, including deformations between additive and multiplicative Hamiltonian geometry and their behavior under reduction.

Quiver moduli and field theories

Symplectic moduli spaces defined by Lax--Kirchhoff equations and their cutting, gluing, and two-dimensional TQFT structures.

Hyperkähler geometry and analysis

Nahm--Kirchhoff moduli spaces, hyperkähler quotients, and variational methods in gauge-theoretic moduli problems.

Recent research

Nahm--Kirchhoff moduli spaces, hyperkähler quotient and 2D field theories
My recent work develops hyperkähler moduli spaces from Nahm--Kirchhoff equations on quivers and studies their field-theoretic structure. arXiv:2610.03384

My work in Poisson geometry is developed in From multiplicative to additive geometry: Deformation theory and 2D TQFT; my work on Dirac structures is developed in Dirac geometry, deformation theory and shifted symplectic geometry.
